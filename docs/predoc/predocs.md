# Administrative

Predocs are academic staff and appointments are typically made through the Center on Food Security and the Environment (FSE) which is a research center inside the Freeman Spogli Institute (FSI). The main administrative contact points are Sonal for day-to-day operations (room key, computer, etc) and Karen for finance and HR topics (contract, salary, performance review, immigration stuff, etc). Once your employment starts at Stanford, the easiest way to get a hold of either of them is on slack. Sonal works part time so is only online a few days a week. Karen is full time and typically responds quickly to inquiries.

All academic staff have year-to-year contracts which means 1 year contract with possibility of renewal subject to funding availability. The reasons contracts are 1-year are administrative and do not reflect how long people actually 
stay. The first year of everybody's contract is technically called a "trial period" so after 12 months you will receive a letter saying your trial period is up though it functionally doesn't change anything. Every year you stay you will need to sign a new contract. Annual raises are determined by HR and are intended to reflect cost of living adjustments.

# Performance Review

Staford requires annual performance reviews tied to their system of goal setting. Essentially you set annual goals within Stanford system and then you self-evaluate and then Marshall evaluates and then you discuss. The goals system is a university-wide requirement. Within lab the requirement is used as an opportunity to check in, discuss personal big picture, and plan out next projects.  


# Graduate School

## Essays and other application materials

If you want someone to review and provide feedback on your essays other lab members are typically happy to do so. You can always ask Sam. Postdocs or grad students are often willing to provide feedback as well. And depending on availability Marshall may provide input as well. Everyone in lab, grad students and above have gone through this process, and are typically happy to help where they can. Ideally 1 week or more lead time is a reasonable request for most people to turn arround comments. More for Marshall. 


## Letters of rec from Marshall

Marshall has a system for letter of rec requests that involves working with him and his (fully remote) assistant Chumi (peraltad@stanford.edu).

Anyone who needs recommendation letters should maintain a Google spreadsheet with two tabs and share it with Chumi and Marshall.

### Tab 1: Applications

Include the following columns:

- Where you are applying
- Position type (e.g., PhD program, postdoc)
- Date the letter request was sent
- Letter due date

Sort applications by letter due date, with the most recent deadline first.

### Tab 2: Letter Materials

Include:

- Link to the most up-to-date personal statement
- Link to your CV
- Bullet points describing anything specific you would like highlighted in the letter that might otherwise be missed

This is not the time to be modest. The goal is to make it as easy as possible to write a strong letter on your behalf.

### Requesting Letters

- Use Marshall's email address when submitting letter requests.
- Chumi has access to Marshall's email and will upload the letters.
- For a **new type of letter** (e.g., Marshall is writing for you for the first time), provide **at least two weeks' notice**, and ideally more.
- For an **additional application of a type for which a letter has already been written** (e.g., another PhD program after the initial graduate-school letter is complete), less lead time is typically needed.
